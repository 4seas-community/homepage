MORE TIME. MORE LIFE.

Care about the length of life,
We also care whether tomorrow
is worth looking forward to.

A body that supports what you want to do.
A curiosity about the world that stays alive.
People you want to be close to.

If machines one day do most of our work, and life sciences give us more time, how will we live? We are used to introducing ourselves through our professions and finding value in being busy. As those habits shift, what deserves our time becomes a question we need to answer for ourselves.

At 4Seas, we begin with shared living. A conversation about movement can lead to training together the next day; a discussion of nutrition can inform the next meal. Sleep Lab’s exploration of environments and records can meet real daily routines. Everyday life gives knowledge a place to be used, while experience and records help us revise our judgement.

As part of the Zuzalu community network, we also make room for builders: people interested in health, researchers, practitioners and technology teams meeting in one place. Individuals can find companions for consistent practice; projects can start with real needs and find people willing to explore together.

Sleep

02 / NUTRITION

Nutrition

03 / MOVEMENT

Movement

Find a suitable intensity and rhythm through available movement sessions and shared practice. Notice how your body feels, and leave room for rest and recovery.

04 / MENTAL WELLBEING

Mental wellbeing

Pay attention to your relationship with yourself, others and life. Make space for quiet, conversation and support. We welcome relevant professionals to help develop further practices.

From “How do I feel?”
to changes you can keep observing.

Build a personal baseline across eight assessment themes, placing measurements in everyday life and long-term trends. Understand the question a measurement answers before choosing the tool.

01

Body composition

Track weight, body fat and skeletal muscle to observe changes in body composition.

02

Vascular health

Explore vascular function through endothelial response and arterial stiffness.

03

Cardiorespiratory fitness

Compare heart rate under a fixed exercise load and recovery after exercise.

04

Metabolic health

Use CGM to observe daily glucose trends alongside food, movement and sleep records.

05

Muscle strength

Explore grip strength, peak force in specific movements and differences between sides.

06

Power and balance

Use jumping and balance tasks to observe ground reaction forces, postural control and side-to-side differences.

07

Reaction and coordination

Explore reaction speed, visual processing and hand–eye coordination.

08

Posture assessment

Visualise body posture and keep comparable records over time.

SLEEP / ENVIRONMENT / PERSONAL DATA

Sleep room

A room that helps you understand your sleep.

The sleep room connects microenvironment records, personal sleep data and coaching. Combine records from the bed and wearables to observe changes in your surroundings and body, gradually finding conditions that suit your rest.

Ask about the sleep room ↗

Connect changes in the room with your sleep.

Each participant has an ongoing personal record. Review environmental data, sleep records from the bed and wearables, and how you feel on waking along the same timeline.

01 / MICROENVIRONMENT

The room’s microenvironment

Record bed temperature, room temperature and humidity, CO₂, particles, light and sound levels to understand the environment in which you sleep.

02 / PERSONAL SLEEP

The bed and wearables

Combine the bed’s records with data from authorised connections to smart rings, watches or bands. Observe personal measures such as sleep duration, overnight heart rate and HRV.

03 / DAILY EXPERIENCE

Morning experience and daily habits

Record your energy and sense of recovery on waking, alongside movement, food and daily routines, to understand the life behind the data.

YOUR SLEEP / PERSONAL GUIDANCE

Sleep coach, helping you understand and improve your sleep.

Every sleep-room participant can receive guidance from a sleep coach. The coach interprets the microenvironment, personal sleep records and morning experience together, helping choose adjustments and updating suggestions as records accumulate.

01

Understand your sleep

Place records from the bed, wearables and microenvironment on the same timeline to understand which changes deserve attention.

02

Find what you can adjust

Start with routines, light, bed temperature, movement and relaxation habits. Work with your coach to choose changes that suit you.

03

Review, then keep improving

Compare environmental and personal sleep records before and after adjustments. Use your feedback to keep updating suggestions and turn useful approaches into sustainable habits.

Every part of the room supports ongoing observation.

From environmental controls to overnight records, each part of the room serves a clear question.

01

Bed temperature control

Adjust bed temperature and compare sleep experiences under different conditions, making temperature a factor you can observe and control.

02

Sleep EEG

Use sleep EEG records to support analysis of sleep stages and overnight changes, retaining raw data for review.

03

Overnight blood oxygen

Record changes in blood oxygen during sleep as clues for further understanding of the night.

04

Air quality

Observe CO₂, particles, temperature and humidity continuously, aligning environmental changes with sleep records on one timeline.

05

Filtered fresh air

Bring in fresh air through filtered ventilation, while monitoring indoor CO₂ and outdoor particles.

06

Night-time sound levels

Record sound intensity and changes without recording conversations.

07

Morning and evening light

Consider morning light exposure and adjustable, low-brightness lighting in the evening.

08

Blackout and morning reflection

Reduce night-time light disturbance and review morning reflections alongside overnight records.

Observe first, then decide what to change.

Establish a baseline combining microenvironment and personal sleep data, then choose one clear adjustment with your coach. Keep recording, comparing and sharing feedback to refine the next step and support improvement in everyday life.

PEPTIDE / HUMAN HEALTH / QUALITY

Peptide：
Explore new possibilities for health through the body’s signals.

Peptides are short chains of amino acids. Many act as messengers in the body, participating in metabolism, appetite, hormonal signalling and tissue repair. Understanding these signals helps us explore how to better support the body’s functions.

Ask about peptides and testing ↗

From what it is to how it affects people.

Our interest in peptides centres on two connected questions: is the sample reliable, and what evidence supports a particular peptide for a specific health goal? Talks, workshops and professional exchanges connect research findings with real personal questions.

01 / METABOLISM

Metabolism and weight management

Use insulin and GLP-1 signalling as examples to understand how peptides participate in glucose and appetite regulation, and how specific peptide medicines are used for relevant metabolic conditions and weight management.

02 / RECOVERY

Repair and physical function

Explore peptide research on tissue repair, inflammatory signalling and recovery from exercise. Compare the questions that human studies, early experiments and personal experience can each answer.

03 / HEALTHSPAN

Healthspan and ongoing observation

Start with specific goals such as energy, physical function and metabolic measures. Discuss the links between peptide research and healthy ageing, considering personal experience alongside ongoing records.

Each peptide has its own effects and evidence. Peptide medicines in clinical use and exploratory research directions are discussed separately.

First, ask: what is actually in the sample?

From identity testing to contaminant analysis, independent quality testing provides clear measurement evidence and traceable reports.

01 / IDENTITY

Identity

Use mass spectrometry and further identification where needed to check whether a sample matches the labelled peptide.

02 / PURITY

Purity and impurities

Use chromatography and related methods to analyse the target substance and impurities, preserving the original measurement evidence.

03 / CONTENT

Actual content

Quantify against appropriate reference standards and report content separately from purity.

04 / ELEMENTS

Heavy metals

Use suitable elemental analysis methods to test for contaminants such as lead, arsenic, cadmium and mercury.

05 / MICROBIOLOGY

Endotoxins and sterility

Endotoxins and sterility are different questions, assessed separately with appropriate methods.

06 / PARTICLES

Particles and microplastics

Particle counting and polymer identification answer two separate questions: how many particles are present, and what material are they made of?

07 / COMPOSITION

Water and counterions

Use water, counterion and other composition information to give a fuller picture of sample quality.

08 / TRACEABILITY

Traceable reports

Keep a reviewable reporting trail from sample intake and method records to raw data and verification of results.

How should we interpret evidence about peptides?

Peptides are a category; each one needs its own evidence. Community Peptide and CGM events ask whether claims come from human research, animal studies, proposed mechanisms or personal experience. We encourage comparison of evidence types, study conditions and what remains unknown.

Product quality and evidence for human use are discussed separately. Testing describes a sample; it does not replace safety and efficacy research. These discussions do not provide personalised medication, dosage or injection instructions.

Method background:USP resources on peptide quality and reference standards；FDA peptide safety information。

We welcome builders in analytical testing, quality systems, data and software, alongside research and industry partners, to explore this direction together.

DATA CENTER / PERSONAL CONTROL / AI ASSISTED

A data center for people and community

Turn records scattered across wearables, test reports and everyday observations into information you can understand and use. Connect them with community environment and event data to give health practices an ongoing basis for reflection.

PTDS / PERSONAL DATA

Start with your own records.

The personal data center, PTDS, brings together wearables, health reports, device readings and manual observations with local-first organisation and management. You decide which sources to connect, what to save and whom to share with.

WearablesTest reportsPersonal observations
CTDS / COMMUNITY DATA

Give records their everyday context.

The community data center, CTDS, brings together records from space sensors, weather, air quality and events. It helps us understand the environment of shared living and provides a data foundation for community services and collaboration.

Spaces and environmentWeather and airCommunity events

Connect as needed, with your permission.

PTDS and CTDS are independent, collaborating through open interfaces and explicit permission. Choose which fields or summaries to share and define the purpose, scope, recipients and expiry. Your original personal records remain under your control.

Turn records into information you can use.

01 / ORGANISE

Collect, organise and review

Organise device readings, reports and manual records into comparable data, retaining original sources, times and processing history. Extracted information is reviewed by you before saving.

02 / COMPARE

Compare your own changes

Bring together records from different times around questions about sleep, activity or metabolism. Status cards and trend comparisons help you and your coach find questions worth observing further.

03 / AI ASSISTED

AI-assisted understanding

AI will use local reading and source traceability to help explain records, organise changes and suggest questions. Data use and sharing remain subject to your permission.

BEFORE YOU COME

Before you visit.

How do I join everyday activities for the first time?

Check the date, time and location, then follow the event instructions. Registration, fees, preparation and other requirements are specific to each event. Venues may differ, so check before leaving.

How can I join Longevity Month?

Explore the key dates above and Contact the Longevity teamto discuss participation and collaboration.

Can I keep taking part in community practice?

Start with an event, or keep recording, sharing and practising around your own health questions. Contact the team to discuss ongoing collaboration with methods, tools or projects.

What mental wellbeing activities are available?

Mental wellbeing is a long-term focus. Community conversation and mutual support make room for quiet, connection and understanding.

Do I need to submit health data?

You decide how health records are saved and shared. Personal and community data are managed separately, with the purpose, scope and recipients defined when you give consent.

Can I bring a project or host an event?

You can bring a clear question, content proposal or project introduction andContact the Longevity team. We welcome collaboration in Biotech, AI, Crypto and other fields, as well as shared practical experience. Venue, outreach, task support and collaboration arrangements are discussed individually.
